Statement

Objects that feel inevitable

I trained as an industrial designer in Kyoto and spent six years shaping injection-moulded housings nobody ever looked at twice. That work taught me a discipline I still use: a form is finished when nothing else can be removed without the object failing.

Since 2023 my practice has been half generative, half manual. I write small programs that produce thousands of variations — contour fields, decay gradients, orbital lattices — then spend days selecting the four or five that hold together. The machine proposes; I dispose.

Everything published here is built as a single source file and adapted by Desmake into whatever the buyer needs: a 70×100 giclée, a DTG tee, a resin vase. I design for the constraint of every adapter simultaneously, which is harder and, I think, more honest.

How I work

01 / SEED
Write the system

A generative sketch in code — noise fields, geometry rules, palette constraints. Typically 400–2,000 outputs per session.

02 / CURATE
Cull hard

Print contact sheets, live with them for a week, keep under 1%. Most of the work is deciding what to throw away.

03 / ADAPT
Prove the object

Sample every adapter before publishing. If the tee fails, the poster does not ship either.
