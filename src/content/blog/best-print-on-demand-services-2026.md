## Hook
Print-on-demand (POD) lets you sell physical products — tees, posters, stickers — without holding stock. But in 2026 the field has split into two camps: manual fulfilment backends you run by hand, and AI-native platforms that merchandise the product for you. Here is how the leading services compare, and which fits a creator who generates art with AI.

## What "best" means for an AI artist
Most POD comparisons score on product catalogue and margins. For someone generating art with Midjourney, DALL·E or Stable Diffusion, three things matter more:
1. **How much manual setup** each design needs before it is a live product.
2. **Whether AI agents** can publish and fulfil on your behalf.
3. **Pricing transparency** — can you see the real manufacturing cost?

## The 2026 landscape at a glance
- **Manual POD backends** (Printful, Printify, Gelato): huge supplier networks, but you build every product/SKU by hand and write the copy yourself.
- **Artist marketplaces** (Redbubble, Society6): upload once, they spread it across products, but you inherit a fixed royalty and no agent channel.
- **AI-native design-to-manufacture** (Desmake): one design is auto-adapted, mockup-rendered, copywritten, priced and listed across six methods — and is MCP/API-operable by agents.

## Side-by-side
- **Setup time per design:** Manual POD = minutes-to-hours per SKU; marketplaces = quick upload but limited control; Desmake = one publish, ~4 minutes to live listing.
- **Product range:** Most cover apparel + posters; Desmake adds phone cases, business cards and 3D prints from the same artwork.
- **Agent commerce:** Only Desmake exposes an MCP server so an AI agent can search, generate, publish and order.
- **Cost visibility:** Desmake shows manufacturing cost, your margin and retail on every order; others keep supplier margins opaque.

## Best for beginners
If you want zero setup and don't mind a fixed royalty, an artist marketplace is the fastest start. If you want control over pricing and a transparent cost breakdown, an AI-native platform beats hand-building products on a manual backend.

## Best for scaling
Once you have more than a handful of designs, manual POD turns merchandising into a second job. Auto-adaptation across six manufacturing methods is the difference between publishing 5 designs a week and 50.

## Why AI-native wins for generated art
Generated art is cheap to make in volume but expensive to merchandise by hand. The bottleneck is never the art — it is the mockups, copy, pricing and listing. A platform that does that pipeline for you changes the economics of selling AI art.

## FAQ
**What is the best print-on-demand service for AI art in 2026?** It depends on how much manual work you want. Manual backends (Printful, Printify) give supplier choice but require you to build each product. AI-native platforms like Desmake auto-adapt one design across posters, apparel, stickers, phone cases, business cards and 3D prints and list it for you.

**Do print-on-demand services allow AI-generated art?** Yes. All major POD services accept AI-generated artwork as long as you own the rights to the image. Check each platform's content policy, and avoid copyrighted characters or trademarked logos.

**Which POD service shows real manufacturing cost?** Desmake shows manufacturing cost, your royalty and the retail price on every order, so you never price blind. Most traditional backends hide supplier margins behind a retail you set.

**Can AI agents sell print-on-demand products?** Only platforms with an agent-commerce layer. Desmake exposes an MCP server so an agent with a scoped key can search the catalogue, publish designs and place orders autonomously.
